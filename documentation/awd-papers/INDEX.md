# AWD Research Papers Index

## Papers Found: 5

### 1. Hoang et al. (2023)
**Title:** Higher rice grain yield and lower methane emission achieved by alternate wetting and drying in central Vietnam  
**File:** `hoang2023_awd_methane_vietnam.md`  
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.eja.2023.126992  
**Key Finding:** AWD reduces CH₄ emissions by 34% while increasing rice yield by 4.4%

### 2. Chu et al. (2015)
**Title:** Alternate wetting and moderate drying increases rice yield and reduces methane emission in paddy field with wheat straw residue incorporation  
**File:** `chu2015_awmd_wheat_straw.md`  
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1002/fes3.66  
**Key Finding:** AWMD reduces CH₄ emissions by 49.8-57.5% while increasing yield

### 3. Dahlgreen & Parr (2024)
**Title:** Exploring the Impact of Alternate Wetting and Drying and the System of Rice Intensification on Greenhouse Gas Emissions: A Review of Rice Cultivation Practices  
**File:** `dahlgreen2024_awd_sri_review.md`  
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.3390/agronomy14020378  
**Key Finding:** Comprehensive review of 41 AWD studies showing consistent methane emission reductions

### 4. Xuan et al. (2025)
**Title:** Advances in mitigating methane emissions from rice cultivation: past, present, and future strategies  
**File:** `xuan2025_methane_mitigation_review.md`  
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1007/s11356-025-36776-8  
**Key Finding:** AWD reduces methane emissions by 41.37% compared to continuous flooding

### 5. Ume (2024)
**Title:** Evaluating the Impact of Digitalized Alternate Wetting and Drying (AWD) Systems on Rice Yield, Water Use Efficiency, and Methane Emissions: A Randomized Controlled Trial  
**File:** `ume2024_digitalized_awd_rct.md`  
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1257/rct.14932  
**Key Finding:** Randomized controlled trial evidence for digitalized AWD systems

## Summary
All papers provide evidence that AWD reduces methane emissions while maintaining or increasing rice yield, supporting the AGRI-GRID carbon credit methodology for rice cultivation. The papers span from 2015 to 2025 and include both field trials and comprehensive reviews.
