# Punjab & Haryana AWD Implementation Results

- **Source:** World Bank, IRRI, Haryana Irrigation Management Institute
- **Year:** 2024
- **URL:** https://blogs.worldbank.org/en/water/what-works-water-conservation-insights-study-haryana-india
- **Type:** Research Study
- **Relevance to AGRI-GRID:** Directly relevant for North India deployment showing 22% reduction in pumping hours and 0.4-0.6 bcm annual water savings potential.

## Summary

A comprehensive study conducted in Haryana, India in collaboration with the Haryana Irrigation Management Institute and Haryana Agricultural University evaluated the effectiveness of AWD training among farmers. The study focused on water conservation in the rice-wheat cropping system that dominates Punjab and Haryana agriculture.

### Study Context
- Groundwater extraction from Northern India accounts for **25% of all groundwater extraction worldwide**
- Rice-wheat cropping system prevalent in both states
- Punjab and Haryana contribute above national average: **4.8 tonnes rice/ha, 6.5 tonnes wheat/ha**

## Key Findings

### Water Savings
- AWD training led to **2.2-hour decline** in pumping during AWD weeks
- **22% decline** in total weekly pumping hours
- Estimated **0.4-0.6 bcm annual water savings** potential
- **7-11% reduction** in government spending on irrigation

### Economic Analysis
- AWD training is more economical than alternatives:
  - Land leveling (capital intensive)
  - Electricity subsidy reduction (politically sensitive)
  - Direct farmer payments for water reduction (expensive)

### Farmer Adoption Factors
- Farmers who own pumps and control water use show higher adoption
- Awareness of water depletion consequences drives behavior change
- Water quality knowledge increasing among farming communities

### State-Level Impact
- Punjab and Haryana produce rice surplus to national needs
- Combined potential to save significant groundwater through AWD adoption
- Training costs minimal compared to infrastructure alternatives

## AGRI-GRID Citation

The Haryana study provides economic evidence for AGRI-GRID's farmer incentive model. The 22% pumping reduction translates directly to energy savings and emissions reduction. The finding that farmers who own pumps adopt AWD better informs AGRI-GRID's targeting strategy for carbon credit eligible farmers.
