# Higher rice grain yield and lower methane emission achieved by alternate wetting and drying in central Vietnam

- **Authors:** Trong Nghia Hoang, Kazunori Minamikawa, Takeshi Tokida, Rota Wagai, Thi Xuan Phuong Tran, Thi Hoang Dong Tran, Dang Hoa Tran
- **Year:** 2023
- **Journal/Source:** European Journal of Agronomy, Volume 151
- **DOI/URL:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.eja.2023.126992
- **Relevance to AGRI-GRID:** This paper provides direct evidence that AWD reduces methane emissions while maintaining or increasing rice yield, supporting the core AWD→methane reduction→carbon credit claim for our project.

## Abstract
Rice is the main staple food for more than half of the world's population, but rice cultivation is a significant source of atmospheric methane (CH₄). Alternate wetting and drying (AWD) reduces CH₄ emission from paddy field, but the effect on rice yield remains unclear. Organic soil amendment increases grain yield but simultaneously increases CH₄ emission. Therefore, the combination of AWD and organic amendment may compensate for each other's shortcomings. The objective of this study was to assess whether AWD and organic amendment can increase rice yield while mitigating CH₄ emission. The experiments were conducted in a farmer's paddy field in Thua Thien Hue province, Vietnam, during five consecutive rice growing seasons in 2019–2021. Two water management practices, continuous flooding (CF) and AWD, with (+O) or without (−O) the application of a commercially available organic fertilizer were examined under normal cultivation conditions. Compared with CF, AWD significantly reduced CH₄ emission by 34%, increased nitrous oxide (N₂O) by 46%, and increased grain yield by 4.4%. The +O treatment significantly increased the yield by 3.7% relative to −O. Relative to CF−O by season, AWD+O significantly increased the yield. The integrated global warming potential of CH₄ and N₂O emissions was decreased by 33% and irrigation water use was reduced by 33% in AWD plots relative to that in CF plots. These results indicate that AWD by itself has the potential to increase rice yield as well as reduce CH₄ emission, and the combination of AWD with organic amendment would ensure yield increase.

## Key Findings
- AWD reduced CH₄ emissions by 34% compared to continuous flooding
- AWD increased rice grain yield by 4.4% compared to continuous flooding
- Combined AWD and organic amendment ensured yield increase while reducing emissions
- Integrated global warming potential decreased by 33% with AWD
- Irrigation water use reduced by 33% in AWD plots
