# Exploring the Impact of Alternate Wetting and Drying and the System of Rice Intensification on Greenhouse Gas Emissions: A Review of Rice Cultivation Practices

- **Authors:** James Dahlgreen, Adam Parr
- **Year:** 2024
- **Journal/Source:** Agronomy, 14(2), 378
- **DOI/URL:** https://doi.org/10.3390/agronomy14020378
- **Relevance to AGRI-GRID:** This comprehensive review synthesizes evidence from 41 AWD studies showing consistent methane emission reductions, providing robust scientific support for AWD-based carbon credit methodologies.

## Abstract
Rice provides ~20% of human dietary energy and, for many people, a similar share of their protein. Rice cultivation, however, produces significant greenhouse gas (GHG) emissions, comparable to those from the aviation sector. The main GHG from rice production is methane, mostly a result of conventional rice cultivation (CRC) keeping rice fields continuously flooded during the crop cycle. There is extensive evidence that alternate wetting and drying (AWD) of rice fields substantially reduces methane emissions. This review examines the impact of AWD and the System of Rice Intensification (SRI) on GHG emissions from rice cultivation.

## Key Findings
- AWD consistently reduces methane emissions across diverse climatic conditions
- SRI practices also show potential for methane emission reductions
- Both methods can maintain or improve rice yields while reducing emissions
- The review provides comprehensive data supporting carbon credit methodologies
- Global adoption of AWD could significantly reduce rice cultivation's climate impact
