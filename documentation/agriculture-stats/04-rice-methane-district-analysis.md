# India Rice Methane Emissions: District-Level Analysis and Mitigation Potential

- **Source:** Multiple peer-reviewed studies: Bhatia et al. (2013), Patange et al. (2024), Sahil et al. (2024)
- **Year:** 2024 (compiled from recent publications)
- **URL:** https://doi.org/10.1002/ghg.1339, https://doi.org/10.1088/1748-9326/ad4e4e, https://doi.org/10.1029/2024gb008107
- **Type:** Scientific Research Compilation
- **Relevance to AGRI-GRID:** District-level methane emission factors and mitigation scenarios for Indian rice cultivation, essential for AGRI-GRID's localized climate-smart agriculture recommendations.

## Summary
Indian rice fields covering 43.86 million hectares across different ecosystems (upland, rainfed, irrigated, deepwater) emitted 3.37 million tonnes of CH₄ (84.25 Tg CO₂ equivalents) in 2007. District-level emissions increased by ~930% from 1966-2017, driven by management practices including animal manure, fertilizer application, and water input. Under current policies, CH₄ emissions from agriculture are projected to reach 18.21 Mt by 2030 and 18.37 Mt by 2050. A sustainable agriculture scenario could reduce CH₄ by 6% by 2030 and 27% by 2050. Uttar Pradesh is the largest state emitter (17% of CH₄), followed by Andhra Pradesh, Rajasthan, West Bengal, and Madhya Pradesh.

## Key Statistics
- Indian rice area under different ecosystems: 43.86 million hectares
- CH₄ emissions from rice paddies (2007): 3.37 Mt CH₄ = 84.25 Tg CO₂ eq
- District-level emissions increase (1966-2017): ~930%
- Agriculture contributes: 74% of CH₄, 72% of N₂O emissions in India
- Rice cultivation: 21% of agricultural methane emissions
- Enteric fermentation: 71% of agricultural methane emissions
- Current policies CH₄ projection: 18.21 Mt (2030), 18.37 Mt (2050)
- Sustainable agriculture scenario reduction: 6% CH₄ by 2030, 27% by 2050
- N₂O emissions from agricultural soils: 118.67 Gg (direct) + 19.48 Gg (indirect)
- Top rice methane emitting states: UP, AP, Rajasthan, West Bengal, MP (>50% of total)
- Intermittent irrigation reduces rice CH₄ by 40%
- Farmyard manure increases rice GWP by 41%
