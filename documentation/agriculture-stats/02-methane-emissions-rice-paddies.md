# Methane Emissions from Indian Rice Paddies

- **Source:** Multiple sources: Global Rice Paddy Inventory (GRPI), UN Global Methane Status Report 2025, IPCC, Springer Nature
- **Year:** 2024-2025 (compiled from multiple studies)
- **URL:** https://doi.org/10.1029/2024ef005479 (GRPI), https://www.downtoearth.org.in/climate-change/cop30-offers-india-a-singular-opportunity-to-address-its-methane-blind-spot
- **Type:** Scientific Research / Emissions Inventory
- **Relevance to AGRI-GRID:** Quantifies methane emissions from rice cultivation critical for AGRI-GRID's climate impact assessment and mitigation pathway modeling.

## Summary
Rice cultivation is a major source of methane (CH₄), a greenhouse gas 86x more potent than CO₂ over 20 years. India is the second-largest rice methane emitter globally. CH₄ emissions from Indian rice fields increased from 3.7 Tg (1966) to 4.8 Tg (2017) driven by area expansion and water-regime variations. The GRPI estimates India's rice methane emissions at 6.5 ± 1.0 Tg a⁻¹ (2022), significantly higher than UNFCCC's 3.4 Tg. Agriculture accounts for ~74% of India's CH₄ and ~72% of N₂O emissions. Rice cultivation contributes ~21% of agricultural methane emissions. A 25% warming reduction requires curtailing animal manure (8.5%), nitrogen fertilizer (12.9%), and water input (10.9%).

## Key Statistics
- India is 2nd largest rice methane emitter globally (6.5 ± 1.0 Tg a⁻¹ in 2022)
- Global rice methane emissions: 39.3 ± 4.7 Tg a⁻¹ (2022)
- China largest emitter: 8.2 ± 1.0 Tg a⁻¹
- Top 5 emitters (China, India, Bangladesh, Vietnam, Thailand) = 78% of global total
- CH₄ from Indian rice fields: 3.7 Tg (1966) → 4.8 Tg (2017)
- Agriculture contributes 74% of India's CH₄ and 72% of N₂O emissions
- Rice cultivation = 21% of agricultural methane emissions
- Enteric fermentation = 71% of agricultural methane emissions
- Intermittent irrigation can reduce rice methane emissions by 40%
- India's rice area: ~44 million hectares (stable for 20 years)
- Rice methane intensity: 10-120 kg CH₄ per tonne rice produced (global mean 51)
