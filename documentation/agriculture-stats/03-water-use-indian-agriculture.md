# Water Use in Indian Agriculture: Consumption, Efficiency, and Projections

- **Source:** Compiled from NCAER, NITI Aayog, CEEW, CWC (Central Water Commission), and academic sources
- **Year:** 2024-2025 (compiled from multiple reports)
- **URL:** https://cwc.gov.in/ (Central Water Commission); https://niaa.gov.in/ (National Irrigation Agency)
- **Type:** Statistical Compilation
- **Relevance to AGRI-GRID:** Central dataset for AGRI-GRID's water-crop nexus analysis, providing agricultural water consumption patterns, efficiency metrics, and future projections.

## Summary
Agriculture is India's largest water consumer, accounting for 76-78% of total water withdrawals. India uses 2-3 times more water per unit of major food crop compared to China, Brazil, and US. Groundwater supplies 62% of irrigation needs. Current irrigation efficiency is only 35-40% (target: 60%). Transitioning to efficient methods (drip, sprinkler) could save up to 146 BCM annually. Total water demand projected to grow from 634 BCM (2000) to 1,447 BCM by 2050. By 2030, water demand may exceed supply by nearly twofold.

## Key Statistics
- Agriculture share of India's water use: 76-78% (vs. global 70%)
- Groundwater share of irrigation: 62%
- 95% of 23.14 million minor irrigation schemes draw groundwater
- Current irrigation efficiency: 35-40%
- Target irrigation efficiency: 60%
- Potential annual water savings with drip/sprinkler: 146 BCM
- Total water demand projections:
  - 2000: 634 BCM
  - 2025: 1,093 BCM
  - 2050: 1,447 BCM
- Water demand may exceed supply by 2x by 2030
- India uses 2-3x more water per unit of major food crop vs. China, Brazil, US
- Agriculture water withdrawal share: 77% (2010) → 81% (2050)
- Tank irrigation area: 46.30 lakh ha (1960-61) → 22.05 lakh ha (2021-22)
- Per capita water availability: 6,042 m³ (1947) → 1,486 m³ (2021)
