# The Water Paradox in India: Managing Abundance Amid Growing Scarcity

- **Source:** NCAER (National Council of Applied Economic Research), Working Paper #182
- **Year:** June 2025
- **URL:** https://ncaer.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/NCAER-WP-182-June-2025.pdf
- **Type:** Working Paper / Policy Research
- **Relevance to AGRI-GRID:** Comprehensive analysis of India's water crisis with sector-level demand projections, groundwater depletion data, and irrigation efficiency metrics critical for AGRI-GRID's water sustainability module.

## Summary
India receives 3,880 BCM of annual precipitation but utilises less than one-third effectively. Per capita water availability has declined 75% from 6,042 m³ (1947) to 1,486 m³ (2021). By 2030, water demand may exceed supply by nearly twofold. Agriculture consumes ~78% of total water withdrawals. Groundwater supports 62% of irrigation, 85% of rural water, and 50% of urban water needs. 22% of groundwater blocks are overexploited. The paper provides end-to-end value chain analysis from source sustainability to end-use efficiency.

## Key Statistics
- India's annual precipitation: 3,880 BCM; usable water: 1,123 BCM (690 BCM surface, 436 BCM groundwater)
- Per capita water availability: 6,042 m³ (1947) → 1,486 m³ (2021) → projected 1,140 m³ by 2050
- Agriculture consumes ~78% of total water withdrawals (projected to reduce to 68% by 2050)
- Groundwater extraction: 239.16 BCM (2022) — India is world's largest groundwater consumer
- 22% of groundwater blocks categorized as overexploited
- Total water demand projected to grow from 634 BCM (2000) to 1,447 BCM by 2050
- Current irrigation efficiency: 35-40% (target: 60%)
- Transitioning to drip/sprinkler could conserve up to 146 BCM annually
- Tank irrigation area declined from 46.30 lakh ha (1960-61) to 22.05 lakh ha (2021-22)
- 38,496 water bodies encroached upon (First Water Bodies Census)
