# Reallocating Water to Grow India

- **Source:** CEEW (Council on Energy, Environment and Water)
- **Year:** 2022
- **URL:** https://www.ceew.in/publications/reallocating-water-to-grow-india
- **Type:** Policy Research Report
- **Relevance to AGRI-GRID:** Quantifies water reallocation potential from irrigation to other sectors without compromising agricultural output, providing economic cost-of-inaction estimates essential for AGRI-GRID's water optimization framework.

## Summary
India uses 2-3 times more water per unit of major food crop compared to China, Brazil, and US. Using stochastic frontier analysis (SFA), the study benchmarks irrigation water productivity across 8 crop categories for 3 representative states. Total water withdrawal in BAU scenario projected to increase from 949 BCM (2010) to 1,058 BCM (2050). Agriculture's share expected to increase from 77% (2010) to 81% (2050). In 2030, 160-389 BCM could be saved and reallocated (20-47% of agricultural water withdrawals). The cost of inaction if irrigation water productivity is not enhanced: INR 48 trillion (USD 869 billion) in 2030 and INR 138 trillion (USD 2,520 billion) in 2050.

## Key Statistics
- India uses 2-3x more water per unit of major food crop vs. China, Brazil, US
- Total water withdrawal: 949 BCM (2010) → 1,058 BCM (2050)
- Agriculture share of water withdrawal: 77% (2010) → 81% (2050)
- Potential water savings in 2030: 160-389 BCM (20-47% of agricultural withdrawals)
- Potential water savings in 2050: 166-403 BCM
- Cost of inaction 2030: INR 48 trillion (USD 869 billion)
- Cost of inaction 2050: INR 138 trillion (USD 2,520 billion)
- India's total usable water: 1,123 BCM (690 BCM surface, 436 BCM groundwater)
- Reservoir capacity: only 258 BCM of 690 BCM surface water potential
