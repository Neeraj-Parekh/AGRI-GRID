# Water Productivity Mapping of Major Indian Crops

- **Source:** NABARD (National Bank for Agriculture and Rural Development) & ICRIER (Indian Council for Research on International Economic Relations)
- **Year:** 2018

- **Type:** Policy Research Report
- **Relevance to AGRI-GRID:** First-of-its-kind district-level water productivity maps for 10 major crops including rice; provides critical data on physical, irrigation, and economic water productivity essential for AGRI-GRID's water-crop optimization model.

## Summary
This landmark study explores whether India's existing cropping patterns align with natural water resource endowments. The report maps water productivity of 10 major crops (rice, wheat, maize, tur, chickpea, sugarcane, cotton, groundnut, rapeseed-mustard, potato) across 640 districts. Key finding: rice in Punjab and sugarcane in Maharashtra are classic examples of highly inefficient and unsustainable water use. The study finds significant misalignment between cropping patterns and water availability, with water-guzzling crops concentrated in water-scarce regions.

## Key Statistics
- Agriculture accounts for 78% of India's total water use
- Paddy, sugarcane, and wheat occupy ~40% of gross cropped area but consume >80% of irrigation water
- 40% of irrigation water comes from groundwater sources
- Among 23.14 million minor irrigation schemes, ~95% draw groundwater
- A CEEW study estimates 20% of irrigation water could be saved by 2030 with moderate water-saving strategies
- Physical Water Productivity (PWP) of rice varies dramatically across states (kg/m³)
- Irrigation Water Productivity (IWP) shows perverse inverse relationship with land productivity in Punjab rice
