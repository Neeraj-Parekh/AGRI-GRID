# India National Water Policy 2012 & Draft 2020 Revisions

- **Source:** Ministry of Jal Shakti, Government of India
- **Year:** 2012 (current), 2020 (draft)
- **URL:** https://pib.gov.in/Pressreleaseshare.aspx?PRID=1782283
- **Type:** Policy Document
- **Relevance to AGRI-GRID:** Provides the regulatory framework for water conservation in agriculture including PMKSY, AMRUT 2.0, and crop diversification incentives that AGRI-GRID aligns with.

## Summary

India's National Water Policy was first adopted in 1987 and revised in 2012. The 2012 policy is currently in effect. A drafting committee was constituted to revise the policy, with a Draft National Water Policy released around 2020 for public consultation.

### National Water Policy 2012 - Key Provisions
- **Priority:** Drinking water > Irrigation > Hydropower > Ecology > Agriculture > Industrial
- **Water as Economic Good:** Emphasizes efficient allocation through pricing and regulation
- **River Basin Management:** Promotes integrated approach at river basin level
- **Groundwater:** Regulation of extraction, registration of wells
- **Water Pricing:** Incentivizes conservation through metering and tiered pricing

### Jal Jeevan Mission (JJM)
- **Target:** Provide potable water to every rural household through tap connection by 2024
- **Budget:** Rs. 3.60 lakh crore
- **Sources:** Groundwater, surface water, rainwater harvesting

### AMRUT 2.0 (Atal Mission for Rejuvenation & Urban Transformation)
- **Launch:** October 1, 2021
- **Coverage:** Universal water supply from 500 cities to all statutory towns
- **Budget:** Rs. 2,77,000 crore (including central share of Rs. 76,760 crore)
- **Focus:** Circular economy of water, recycle/reuse of treated sewage

### SahiFasal Campaign
- Launched in 2019 to nudge farmers in water-stressed areas
- Promotes crops that are not water-intensive but economically remunerative
- Workshops conducted in Punjab (Amritsar), Haryana (Kurukshetra), Maharashtra (Aurangabad)

### Jal Shakti Abhiyan (JSA)
- Campaign for water conservation in 256 water-stressed districts
- Focus on rainwater harvesting structures before monsoon

## Key Findings

- **Water Stress:** India has 18% of global population but only 4% of freshwater resources
- **Agriculture Use:** 76-78% of India's water consumed by agriculture
- **Irrigation Efficiency:** Current 35-40% (target 60% with drip/sprinkler)
- **Policy Direction:** Shift from supply augmentation to demand management
- **State Subject:** Water management primarily under State Government jurisdiction

### Draft 2020 Revisions (Key Proposals)
- **Integrated Water Resources Management (IWRM)** approach
- **Water Security Plans** at district/block level
- **Climate Change Adaptation** integration
- **Gender Mainstreaming** in water governance
- **Participatory Irrigation Management (PIM)** promotion

## AGRI-GRID Citation

The National Water Policy provides the regulatory justification for AGRI-GRID's water conservation approach. The SahiFasal campaign directly supports AWD adoption by promoting water-efficient crops. AGRI-GRID's water savings documentation aligns with PMKSY objectives and can be integrated with government water accounting frameworks for validation.
