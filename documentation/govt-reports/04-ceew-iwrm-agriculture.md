# Integrated Water Resources Management and Climate Change Adaptation in Agriculture

- **Source:** CEEW (Council on Energy, Environment and Water)
- **Year:** 2025
- **URL:** https://www.ceew.in/publications/integrated-water-resources-management-and-climate-change-adaptation-in-agriculture
- **Type:** Policy Research Report
- **Relevance to AGRI-GRID:** Analyzes 16 Indian policy instruments for agricultural water management including IWRM and climate change adaptation, directly relevant to AGRI-GRID's policy recommendation framework.

## Summary
Agriculture accounts for 76% of India's total water use (higher than the global average of 70%). This report analyzes policy narratives of 16 instruments from India (including Bihar and Odisha subnational policies) and 6 international policies. Key findings: Indian agriculture heavily relies on groundwater due to decentralized ease of access, is plagued by low water productivity, and has skewed incentives favoring water-intensive crops. Despite MSPs for 22 crops, only wheat, rice, and sugarcane have assured procurement, creating perverse incentives. A CEEW study estimates that with micro-irrigation, mulching, water auditing, and volumetric pricing, 20% of irrigation water could be saved by 2030 and up to 47% by 2050.

## Key Statistics
- India's agricultural water use: 76% of total (vs. global 70%)
- 40% of irrigation water comes from groundwater
- 95% of 23.14 million minor irrigation schemes draw groundwater
- Potential water savings: 20% by 2030 (moderate strategy), 47% by 2050 (rigorous strategy)
- Only wheat, rice, and sugarcane have assured procurement despite MSPs for 22 crops
